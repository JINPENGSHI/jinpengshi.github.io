## Returns to Skills, Technological Change, and Intergenerational Mobility

Working paper · November 2026

<!-- EDIT: Replace this URL if your paper link changes -->
[Read the paper (PDF)](https://drive.google.com/file/d/1jcb92jgaxq7S2iYNGtJRTbdNkTkG7q80/view?usp=drive_link)


### Key findings

- **Rising returns, offsetting occupational sorting.** In our
  benchmark, the return to cognitive skill requirement rises from 0.212 to 0.351,
  while the parental-income and cognitive skill gradient falls from 0.488 to 0.310. The  intergenerational income elasticity stay 
  stable.

- **A small measured contribution from changes within occupations.**
  Dynamic occupational requirements reveal small within-occupation
  contributions to intergenerational income persistence. Even within occupation task upgrading is substatial,
  relative skill requirement ranking across occupations stay unchanged.

- **Large differences in access remain.** Among children born in the 1980s,
  11.8% from the lowest parental-income quintile reach the highest cognitive
  occupational group, compared with 45.3% from the highest quintile, using
  the child-period occupational ranking.
